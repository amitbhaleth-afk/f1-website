The UPSC Study Desk.

A topic-by-topic reference for your preparation. Open any topic to see what it means, what to study, which books can help, useful video sources, and where to practise. Use it as a map, not as a substitute for the official syllabus or your own notes.

Start here: UPSC has two Prelims papers (GS Paper I and qualifying CSAT), followed—if you qualify—by Mains, which includes Essay, four General Studies papers, two papers in one optional subject, and qualifying language papers, followed by the Personality Test. Mains marks used for ranking total 1,750; the interview is 275. Always check the notification for your examination year.

Stage 1

Prelims: General Studies and CSAT

Prelims uses objective multiple-choice questions. GS Paper I determines the cut-off; CSAT Paper II is qualifying, with the current rule requiring at least 33%. Both papers must be attempted. Check the current notification for exact rules.

GS I · 200 marks
CSAT · 200 marks
History of India and the National MovementAncient, medieval, modern India, reform movements and freedom struggle

What it covers: Major developments from early Indian history to the colonial period; social and religious change; British expansion and policies; resistance, reform, nationalism, and the road to independence. For Prelims, build chronology and identify people, places, institutions and events. For Mains, practise explaining causes, effects, continuity and change.

Study checklist

  • Ancient and medieval foundations: political formations, religions, art, architecture and cultural developments.
  • Modern India: European arrival, Company rule, economic and social impact, 1857, reform movements and national organisations.
  • National movement: major phases, mass movements, leaders, ideological differences and constitutional developments.
  • Use timelines and compare movements rather than memorising disconnected dates.

Books and notes

  • NCERT History textbooks, selected by syllabus
  • Rajiv Ahir, A Brief History of Modern India (Spectrum)
  • NCERT Fine Arts textbook for culture topics

Videos and practice

  • Drishti IAS — Modern History search
  • NCERT History lecture search
  • UPSC previous-year papers
Art and CultureArchitecture, sculpture, paintings, literature, music, dance and traditions

What it covers: The features and historical context of Indian cultural traditions. Learn how a form developed, where it is associated, its distinctive features, and how it changed over time. Connect culture to the period and region rather than studying it as an isolated list.

Study checklist

  • Temple and Indo-Islamic architecture, stupas, caves, sculpture and painting traditions.
  • Classical and folk dances, music traditions, theatre, literature and major cultural institutions.
  • UNESCO World Heritage Sites and cultural properties in current affairs.

Books and notes

  • NCERT An Introduction to Indian Art (Class XI)
  • CCRT cultural resources
  • Make a table: form, region, features, period, example.

Study links

  • Centre for Cultural Resources and Training
  • Art and culture video lessons
Geography: India and the WorldPhysical, social and economic geography; maps, climate and resources

What it covers: The physical processes that shape Earth, the distribution of people and resources, and how climate, landforms, water, agriculture, industry and transport influence life. Learn concepts first, then practise maps and link them to news.

Study checklist

  • Earth structure, plate tectonics, earthquakes, volcanoes, landforms and geomorphology.
  • Atmosphere, pressure, winds, monsoon, cyclones, ocean currents and climate.
  • Indian rivers, soils, crops, minerals, energy resources, industries and population patterns.
  • World and India map practice: straits, seas, mountains, rivers, countries and places in news.

Books and notes

  • NCERT Geography Classes VI–XII, progressively
  • G.C. Leong, Certificate Physical and Human Geography (selectively)
  • Atlas for daily map practice

Videos and sources

  • NCERT Geography lectures
  • India Meteorological Department
  • Map-practice lessons
Indian Polity and GovernanceConstitution, political system, Panchayati Raj, public policy and rights

What it covers: The rules and institutions through which India is governed. Understand the Constitution's structure, the powers and limits of institutions, the relationship between Union and states, and the rights and duties of citizens.

Study checklist

  • Constitutional history, Preamble, citizenship, Fundamental Rights, Directive Principles and Fundamental Duties.
  • President, Prime Minister, Council of Ministers, Parliament, Supreme Court and High Courts.
  • Federalism, Governor, emergency provisions, local government and constitutional/statutory bodies.
  • Public policy, transparency, rights issues, elections and current constitutional debates.

Books and notes

  • M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity
  • NCERT Political Science Classes IX–XII
  • Read the relevant Constitution articles when needed.

Videos and sources

  • PRS Legislative Research — bills and Parliament
  • Drishti IAS polity lessons
  • Constitution of India
Economy and Social DevelopmentGrowth, poverty, inclusion, demographics, sustainable development and schemes

What it covers: How an economy produces and distributes goods and services, how government and the central bank influence it, and how economic changes affect people. Start with definitions and relationships before reading complex reports.

Study checklist

  • GDP and growth, inflation, unemployment, poverty, inequality and human development.
  • Money, banking, RBI, monetary policy, fiscal policy, taxation, budget and public debt.
  • Balance of payments, exchange rates, trade, investment and financial inclusion.
  • Agriculture, industry, infrastructure, social-sector schemes and sustainable development.

Books and notes

  • NCERT Economics Classes IX–XII
  • One beginner-friendly Indian Economy reference; avoid collecting several books at once.
  • Economic Survey and Union Budget summaries after basics.

Official sources

  • Reserve Bank of India
  • Union Budget
  • Beginner economy video lessons
Environment, Ecology and BiodiversityEcosystems, species, protected areas, pollution and climate change

What it covers: How living things interact with each other and their environment, and how human activity changes those systems. Understand core ecology terms first; use current affairs to make conservation issues concrete.

Study checklist

  • Food chains, food webs, ecological pyramids, habitats, succession and biomes.
  • Biodiversity, endemic and threatened species, national parks, sanctuaries and biosphere reserves.
  • Pollution, environmental impact assessment, climate change, carbon cycle and international conventions.
  • Conservation policies, renewable energy, waste management and sustainable development.

Books and notes

  • NCERT Biology ecology chapters
  • Shankar IAS, Environment (use selectively and update facts)
  • Create species, convention and protected-area tables.

Official sources

  • Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change
  • Environment video lessons
General Science and TechnologyEveryday science, space, IT, biotechnology, health and emerging technologies

What it covers: Basic scientific principles and their real-world applications. Questions often focus on what a technology does, its uses, limitations and public impact rather than specialist engineering mathematics.

Study checklist

  • School-level physics, chemistry and biology; common diseases, vaccines and biotechnology.
  • Space missions, satellites, remote sensing, AI, semiconductors, telecom and cybersecurity basics.
  • Energy technologies, nuclear science, intellectual property and technology ethics.
  • For each news item, write: principle, use, benefit, risk and Indian context.

Books and notes

  • NCERT Science Classes VI–X and selected XI–XII chapters
  • Science and technology current-affairs notes
  • Do not study advanced engineering detail unless the syllabus or question requires it.

Official sources

  • ISRO
  • Press Information Bureau
  • Science and technology lessons
Current AffairsNational and international events linked to the syllabus

What it covers: News is not a separate memory contest. UPSC-relevant current affairs usually connect to polity, economy, international relations, environment, science, social issues or government policy. Study the issue behind the headline.

A repeatable daily method

  • Choose one reliable newspaper; read selectively rather than every page.
  • For each important issue, note background, why it is in news, key facts, challenges, government action and possible way forward.
  • Use monthly compilations only to fill gaps and revise; do not maintain multiple competing compilations.
  • Verify government data and announcements from primary sources.

Reading sources

  • The Hindu or The Indian Express — choose one
  • PIB releases
  • PRS India policy explainers

Video explainers

  • Sansad TV — Perspective and discussions
  • Daily current-affairs analysis
CSAT: Comprehension and ReasoningQualifying paper: comprehension, logic, problem-solving and analytical ability

What it covers: Reading passages accurately, identifying assumptions and conclusions, solving logical problems, and making reasoned decisions. It is qualifying, but it can be difficult if ignored until the final weeks.

Study checklist

  • Practise passage-based questions and identify the author's main point, inference and tone.
  • Study syllogisms, arrangements, directions, relations, statements and conclusions as needed.
  • Practise basic numeracy: percentages, ratios, averages, profit and loss, time-work, speed-distance and number relations.
  • Learn tables, graphs, charts and data interpretation; review every wrong answer.

Books and practice

  • NCERT Mathematics Classes VI–X for basics
  • UPSC CSAT previous-year papers, timed
  • One aptitude question bank if you need extra practice.

Video lessons

  • CSAT comprehension, reasoning and numeracy
  • Official CSAT papers
Mains · Paper I

GS I: Culture, History, Society and Geography

Answers should explain causes, patterns and consequences, and use relevant examples, maps or diagrams where they genuinely help.

250 marks
Indian Heritage and CultureArt forms, literature and architecture from ancient to modern times

Describe cultural forms with context: origin or period, region, key characteristics, evolution and significance. Avoid writing only lists of names. A small labelled diagram or comparison table can make an answer clearer.

Books

  • NCERT An Introduction to Indian Art
  • CCRT resources for selected traditions

Online resources

  • CCRT
  • GS I culture answer-writing videos
Modern Indian History and Freedom StruggleMid-eighteenth century onwards, significant events, personalities and issues

Go beyond chronology. For a movement or reform, explain the background, aims, methods, participation, achievements, limitations and long-term impact. Compare phases of nationalism and the roles of different social groups.

Books

  • Spectrum, A Brief History of Modern India
  • Relevant NCERT history textbooks

Practice

  • GS I previous-year questions
  • Modern history answer-writing lessons
World HistoryIndustrial Revolution, world wars, redrawing of borders, colonisation and decolonisation

Build cause-and-effect timelines. For each major event, understand economic, political and ideological causes, the key turning points, and the consequences for states and societies. Connect developments to changes in world order.

Books

  • Relevant NCERT world-history chapters
  • One concise world-history reference; keep notes chronological.

Practice

  • World-history lessons
  • Previous-year questions
Indian SocietyDiversity, women, population, poverty, urbanisation, globalisation and social empowerment

Study how social structures and change affect people's lives. Organise answers around causes, effects, groups affected, examples, existing responses, gaps and practical measures. Use current examples, but avoid unsupported statistics.

Books

  • NCERT Sociology Classes XI–XII
  • Class XII Indian Society and Social Change and Development in India

Sources

  • Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation
  • Indian society lectures
Geography and Geophysical PhenomenaResources, industries, earthquakes, tsunamis, volcanoes, cyclones and geographical features

Explain spatial patterns and processes: why something occurs in a place, what controls it, how it affects people and the environment, and how risks can be reduced. Use maps and labelled diagrams when useful.

Books

  • NCERT Geography Classes XI–XII
  • G.C. Leong selectively and a good atlas

Sources

  • IMD
  • National Disaster Management Authority
  • Geography diagrams and answer writing
Mains · Paper II

GS II: Polity, Governance, Social Justice and IR

This paper rewards constitutional understanding and balanced analysis of institutions, public policy, welfare and India's relationships with other countries.

250 marks
Constitution, Federalism and Political SystemConstitution, amendments, basic structure, Union–state relations, Parliament and judiciary

Understand not just what each institution is, but its powers, checks, accountability and practical challenges. For federalism, examine constitutional provisions, fiscal and administrative relations, disputes and cooperative mechanisms.

Books

  • M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity
  • NCERT Political Science Classes XI–XII
  • Constitution of India for selected articles

Sources

  • PRS India
  • Constitution text
  • GS II polity lessons
Governance, Transparency and AccountabilityE-governance, citizen charters, civil services, accountability and public policy

Governance concerns how public decisions are made and implemented. Learn the purpose and limits of tools such as RTI, social audits, grievance redress, service delivery systems and citizen participation. In answers, connect reforms to measurable outcomes and inclusion.

Books and references

  • NCERT Political Science and relevant governance chapters
  • Second Administrative Reforms Commission reports, selected summaries

Sources

  • Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances
  • RTI Online portal
  • Governance answer-writing lessons
Social Justice and Welfare SchemesHealth, education, poverty, nutrition and support for vulnerable sections

Study the gap between a policy's objective and its implementation. For each important scheme, note the target group, benefits, delivery mechanism, responsible ministry, implementation challenges and ways to improve access.

Study materials

  • NCERT sociology and political science chapters
  • Government scheme summaries and budget documents

Sources

  • PIB
  • Union Budget
  • Social justice lessons
International RelationsIndia and its neighbourhood, bilateral relations, global groupings and institutions

Understand interests, geography, trade, security, diaspora, energy and shared challenges behind India's relationships. Track important developments, but make country-wise notes concise and analytical rather than collecting every headline.

Books and notes

  • NCERT Contemporary World Politics
  • One country/organisation fact sheet per major relationship

Sources

  • Ministry of External Affairs
  • International-relations explainers
Mains · Paper III

GS III: Economy, Environment, Technology and Security

Use concepts, current examples and balanced analysis. A strong answer usually identifies the issue, explains its causes and effects, evaluates responses and proposes feasible improvements.

250 marks
Indian Economy, Budgeting and Inclusive GrowthPlanning, resource mobilisation, growth, employment, fiscal policy and infrastructure

Understand how growth, jobs, inflation, investment, taxation, government spending and public debt interact. Read reports after learning the underlying concepts; keep a small glossary and update important indicators from official releases.

Books

  • NCERT Economics Classes IX–XII
  • One standard Indian Economy book, used consistently
  • Economic Survey and Union Budget summaries

Official sources

  • Budget and Economic Survey
  • RBI
  • MoSPI statistics
Agriculture and Food SecurityCrops, irrigation, storage, marketing, MSP, PDS, food processing and technology

Study the farm-to-consumer chain: inputs and productivity, climate and water, procurement and markets, storage and transport, nutrition and food access. Discuss farmer incomes and sustainability alongside production.

Study materials

  • NCERT Geography and Economics chapters on agriculture
  • Government agriculture and food-security scheme notes

Sources

  • Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare
  • Department of Food and Public Distribution
  • Agriculture topic lessons
Science, Technology and Intellectual PropertyDevelopments, applications, indigenisation, AI, space, biotech and digital systems

Explain technologies at a generalist level: how they work in broad terms, where they are used, what benefits they offer, and what risks or governance questions they raise. Connect research and innovation to society and India's capabilities.

Books and notes

  • NCERT Science and selected senior-secondary chapters
  • Short notes on current technology issues

Official sources

  • ISRO
  • Department of Science and Technology
  • PIB
Environment, Climate Change and Disaster ManagementConservation, environmental impact, climate risks, preparedness and resilience

Understand the risk cycle: prevention and mitigation, preparedness, response and recovery. For environmental issues, balance ecological protection, livelihoods, development needs and long-term resilience using concrete examples.

Books

  • NCERT ecology chapters
  • Shankar IAS Environment, selectively
  • NDMA guidelines on selected hazards

Sources

  • NDMA
  • MoEFCC
  • IMD
Internal Security and CybersecurityExtremism, border management, organised crime, cyber threats and security agencies

Study the nature of threats, why they arise, how they affect governance and citizens, and the legal, technological, intelligence and community-based responses used to address them. Keep the focus on public policy and prevention.

Study materials

  • One concise internal-security reference
  • Current-affairs notes on cyber governance and border management

Sources

  • Ministry of Home Affairs
  • CERT-In
  • Internal-security lessons
Mains · Paper IV

GS IV: Ethics, Integrity and Aptitude

This paper tests ethical reasoning and practical judgement. Clear definitions, relevant examples and a reasoned approach to dilemmas matter more than memorising impressive quotes.

250 marks
Ethics, Human Values and AttitudeEthical concepts, moral thinkers, attitude, aptitude and emotional intelligence

Learn to define concepts precisely and distinguish related ideas, such as ethics and law, empathy and sympathy, or integrity and honesty. Use simple real-world examples. Emotional intelligence includes recognising and managing emotions and responding constructively to others.

Books

  • Lexicon for Ethics, Integrity and Aptitude (selectively)
  • NCERT Psychology chapters for basic concepts
  • Your own glossary of definitions and examples

Practice

  • GS IV previous-year papers
  • Ethics concept and case-study lessons
Probity in Governance and Case StudiesPublic service values, accountability, transparency, corruption and ethical dilemmas

For a case study, identify stakeholders, facts, competing values, legal obligations, options and likely consequences. Choose a feasible course of action and explain how it protects public interest while respecting fairness, transparency and due process.

A simple case-study framework

  • Facts and ethical issues: what is known, what is uncertain, and what values conflict?
  • Stakeholders: citizens, vulnerable groups, colleagues, institution and public interest.
  • Options: list realistic choices with benefits, risks and ethical costs.
  • Decision: select an action, explain why, and include safeguards, communication and follow-up.

Books and practice

  • Lexicon for Ethics (selected chapters)
  • Previous-year GS IV case studies
  • Practise one case study weekly at first, then increase frequency.

Sources

  • DARPG
  • GS IV case-study answer writing
Mains · Essay

Essay paper

Essay writing tests whether you can build a clear, coherent argument, explore different dimensions and sustain a logical flow from introduction to conclusion.

250 marks
Essay planning and answer structureIdeas, thesis, dimensions, examples, counterarguments and conclusion

Read the topic carefully and define its key terms. Decide your central argument, then plan a balanced structure. A useful approach is to explore social, political, economic, ethical, scientific, environmental and historical dimensions where relevant—not to force every dimension into every essay.

Practice method

  • Spend time brainstorming before writing; select a clear thesis and logical sequence.
  • Use examples, constitutional values, history, science or personal observations where relevant and accurate.
  • Address a reasonable counterpoint and explain the limits of your argument.
  • Conclude by answering the topic directly and offering a grounded way forward.
  • Write full essays under timed conditions and revise for clarity, repetition and paragraph flow.

Study resources

  • UPSC previous-year essay topics
  • Read well-written editorials for argument structure, not for copying sentences.
  • Maintain a small bank of examples, case studies and quotations you understand.

Practice links

  • Official essay papers
  • Essay structure and practice lessons
Qualifying papers

English and Indian-language papers

These papers are qualifying and their marks are not added to the final merit total. Check the current notification carefully for the applicable language requirements and any exemptions.

English qualifying paperComprehension, précis, usage and clear written expression as specified

Do not assume the paper requires advanced literature. Practise the specific question types shown in recent papers: comprehension, précis or summary writing, vocabulary and grammar-related tasks as applicable. Focus on accurate, concise writing.

Practice

Language-paper rules can include exemptions, so verify rather than relying on informal summaries.

Mains · Optional

One optional subject, two papers

You choose one optional subject and prepare both of its 250-mark papers. Your degree does not force you to select the matching subject. Compare interest, syllabus, past papers, resource availability and time before deciding.

500 marks total
How to choose an optional subjectCompare syllabus size, personal interest, answer style and available sources

Do not choose only because someone scored highly in a subject. Download the official syllabus and at least several years of past papers for each subject on your shortlist. Read sample questions and compare them with your interests and strengths.

Subjects to compare

  • Humanities and social sciences: Sociology, Anthropology, Political Science and International Relations, Geography, History, Philosophy, Public Administration, Psychology, Economics and Law.
  • Science and engineering: Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, Statistics, Geology, Civil Engineering, Electrical Engineering and Mechanical Engineering.
  • Other options include Agriculture, Animal Husbandry and Veterinary Science, Botany, Medical Science, Commerce and Accountancy, Management, Zoology, and literature of an eligible language.

Decision checklist

  • Can I stay interested in the subject for months?
  • Can I understand the syllabus without excessive outside coaching?
  • Are good books, notes and previous-year solutions available?
  • Do I understand what a good answer to a sample question should contain?
